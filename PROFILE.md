### The March deadline is a hard filter, not a preference

Sam begins a study-abroad semester in **Japan on 1 April 2027**. Anything that cannot finish by
**mid-March** is disqualified, regardless of how good it otherwise looks. This kills:

- Most US "Spring" internships, which run **January–April or January–May**
- All co-op programs on a January–June cycle
- Masa / Career Israel (4–6 months)
- Anything summer-cycle in either hemisphere

**Flexibility of the end date is therefore a first-class scoring dimension**, tracked as its own
column. Small startups score High (you set the dates). Structured corporate programs score Low.

**Disclose the end date in the first conversation, every time.** A firm date is a feature to a
startup scoping a ten-week project and a dealbreaker only at programs that were never a fit.
Discovering it in February is what damages a reference.

This is the single most filtering criterion. The overwhelming majority of internships
are summer-cycle. Off-cycle roles exist but are rarely surfaced by aggregators, which
is why the search leans on startups, direct careers pages, and VC portfolio boards.

**Enrollment note:** Because I'm staying enrolled rather than taking leave, I need roles
that either (a) don't require proof of full-time non-enrollment, or (b) tolerate a
student concurrently taking async classes. Most startups won't care. Some structured
programs at large firms explicitly require leave — those get flagged in `jobs.md`.

## Location Feasibility

Verdicts as of 2026-08-02. Re-verify before acting — immigration rules and program
calendars change.

### 🇺🇸 NYC / SF / Austin — `OPEN`
No constraints. Full-depth search. These should carry the bulk of the pipeline.

### 🇦🇺 Australia — `OPEN` *(genuinely — this was the surprise)*

US citizens **are** eligible for the **Work and Holiday visa (subclass 462)**.

- Age 18–30 ✅ (Sam qualifies)
- Requires having completed or being **partway through tertiary education** ✅ (junior year qualifies)
- **No government support letter required for US citizens**; English test waived
- 12 months in Australia, full work rights, **6-month limit per employer** — a Jan–Mar
  stint sits comfortably inside that
- Cost ~AUD $640
- **Annual cap ~2,500 places for the USA.** Once hit, applications close until the new
  program year. This is a real risk — apply early, don't leave it to December.

**Action:** verify current processing times against a January start before committing.
This is the highest-upside international market and should be searched at full depth.

### 🇪🇸 Spain (Madrid / Barcelona) — `PROGRAM-ONLY, HIGH FRICTION`

The legal path is a **student internship visa** backed by a *convenio de prácticas* — a
legally binding **tripartite** agreement signed by three parties: Sam, the Spanish
company, **and UT Austin**.

- Valid up to 6 months, renewable once ✅ duration works
- Internship must be **in the same academic field and qualification level** as the
  degree — economics/stats to a data or business role should satisfy this ✅
- Requires proof of enrollment ✅ (Sam is staying enrolled — this actually *helps* here)
- Since *Real Decreto 1155/2024* (in force May 2025), paperwork is simplified but
  **social security enrollment is now mandatory for every intern in Spain**

**The blocker is UT Austin.** The university must be willing to sign. Many US
universities will only execute a convenio for students enrolled in a formal study-abroad
or credit-bearing internship program.

**Action required from Sam:** contact UT Austin's study abroad / Texas Global office and
ask directly whether they will sign a convenio de prácticas for a self-arranged
internship in Spain, and what lead time they need. **Do this early — this is the long
pole.** If the answer is no, Spain drops off the list and that effort reallocates to
Australia and the US markets.

### 🇯🇵 Japan (Tokyo) — `OPEN, UNPAID ONLY` *(and strategically the strongest option)*

Sam relocates to Japan on 1 April 2027 regardless. That makes Tokyo arguably the most logical
market on the list — but one constraint shapes everything.

- **Japan has no working-holiday agreement with the United States.** Unlike Australia, there is
  no self-sponsored route to paid work.
- **Paid** internships require a **Designated Activities** visa, sponsored by the employer. Few
  companies will run that process for a ten-week stint.
- **Unpaid** internships of **90 days or less**, for students enrolled at an overseas university,
  can be done on **Temporary Visitor** status. **4 Jan – 12 Mar is 68 days — comfortably inside.**
- Longer unpaid arrangements use a *Cultural Activities* visa; not needed at this length.
- Once on a student visa from April, part-time work up to **28 hours/week** is permitted with
  permission — so a January unpaid stint **can convert to paid part-time work at the same company
  in spring**.

**Why unpaid is worth it here specifically:** no second relocation, a network built before the
semester starts, a Japanese company on the résumé while studying there, and a conversion path to
paid work in April. Few unpaid roles clear that bar.

**Positioning:** lead with the April relocation. It removes their biggest objections at once — no
visa sponsorship, no housing, no flight risk, and someone who remains in-country afterward. Target
companies with overseas ambitions, where a native English speaker is a concrete asset. Don't claim
Japanese ability; English-first companies exist and are the right target.

**Sources to work:** [Coral Capital](https://coralcap.co/) (Tokyo VC publishing in English, portfolio
job board — best entry point), [Japan Dev](https://japan-dev.com/), [TokyoDev](https://www.tokyodev.com/),
[iAgora Tokyo](https://www.iagora.com/work/en/jobs-and-internships/for-english-speakers/japan/tokyo).
Roughly 70% of Japanese startups are headquartered in Tokyo.

## Positioning — how I pitch myself

Three narratives to lead with, depending on the role:

**1. Quantitative rigor + commercial instinct.**
Economics with a stats & DS minor is an unusually good combination for growth, analytics,
and BD roles. Most BD/sales candidates can't run a regression; most data candidates can't
hold a customer conversation. The pitch is doing both — reading the numbers *and* the
room. Lead with this for growth, BD, and partnerships.

**2. Comfort with ambiguity, wants the hustle.**
Explicitly seeking an early-stage environment, not a rotational program with a defined
track. For seed/Series A startups this is the thing they're actually screening for —
someone who'll pick up undefined work. Lead with this for startup applications, and pair
it with a concrete example from the resume once uploaded.

**3. Human-centered, not just technical.**
Interest in AI and tech from an applied/human angle rather than a research angle. Fits
product, partnerships, and customer-facing analytics. Positions the non-engineering
background as a deliberate choice rather than a gap. Lead with this for PM and
partnerships roles.

**Off-cycle is an advantage, not an apology.** Startups are chronically understaffed in
Q1 and the applicant pool in January is a fraction of the summer pool. Frame availability
as solving a problem for them.

## Fit Scoring Rubric

*Provisional until resume upload. Scores in `jobs.md` carrying a `~` are estimates from
this profile only.*

**Difficulty (1–5)** — how hard to get in. Higher = harder.
Inputs: brand recognition, expected applicant volume vs. headcount, requirements above
junior-year level, whether it's a known feeder program.

**Fit (1–5)** — how well the profile matches stated qualifications. Higher = better.

**Priority** — composite of Fit, in-person bonus, pay, and deadline urgency.
Drives the default sort so the top of the table is the actual next action.
